# Mentor and reviewer acknowledgements

Spec §49. A template. **No names appear in this repository**, and none appears here.

**Status: empty by design.** No mentor and no reviewer has been acknowledged, because none
has yet been asked, reviewed, or given permission.

---

## Rules

1. **Permission first, in writing, every time.** Nobody is named without having agreed to be
   named, for this specific artefact. Agreement to review is not agreement to be
   acknowledged, and agreement to be acknowledged once is not agreement to appear on
   everything afterwards.
2. **An acknowledgement is not an endorsement, and must not read like one.** "I am grateful
   to X for reviewing the assumptions" is fine. "Reviewed by X" or "validated by X" is not,
   because it transfers responsibility for the claims to someone who did not make them.
3. **Say what they actually did.** Vague thanks invite the reader to assume more involvement
   than there was. Naming the specific contribution protects both parties.
4. **Anonymous is the default.** If someone is unsure, or does not reply, they are thanked
   without a name or not at all. Never name someone by inference from a role ("a professor
   at X").
5. **Do not acknowledge an AI here.** The AI disclosure is a separate, substantive document
   (`authorship_and_ai_disclosure.md`) and putting a model in an acknowledgements list
   trivialises a disclosure that needs to be read.
6. **Do not acknowledge someone to borrow their standing.** If the only reason a name is in
   the list is that it looks good on a submission, it comes out.
7. **Institutions are not thanked unless they actually provided something** (equipment,
   access, time). A school is not acknowledged for existing.

## Template

> ### Acknowledgements
>
> I am grateful to `<name>` for `<specific contribution: reviewing the assumptions and scope;
> criticising the CFD validation approach; questioning me on the results>` on `<date>`. The
> criticism and my responses are recorded in `docs/external_reviews/`.
>
> `<repeat per reviewer>`
>
> `<If applicable:>` I am grateful to `<name or organisation>` for `<access to X, use of Y,
> time on Z>`.
>
> Responsibility for every claim in this work, and for every error in it, is mine.

The last line is not a formality. It is the sentence that makes the rest of the section safe
to write.

## Permission request, to send after a review

> Hello `<name>`,
>
> Thank you again for going through the project on `<date>`. I have written up your comments
> and my responses in the repository, including the two I could not act on and why.
>
> Two separate questions, and no is an easy answer to either:
>
> 1. May I acknowledge you by name in the write-up? It would read: "I am grateful to
>    `<name>` for `<contribution>`." Nothing would describe the work as reviewed, validated
>    or endorsed by you, and nothing you said would be quoted as an endorsement.
> 2. If you would rather not be named, may I say that the work was reviewed externally,
>    without identifying you?
>
> If the answer to both is no, that is completely fine and nothing will mention the review at
> all.
>
> `<student name>`
