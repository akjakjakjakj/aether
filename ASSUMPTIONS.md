# Assumptions

Every assumption that could change a conclusion, with its justification and the
direction of the error it introduces. An assumption that is not written here is a
defect, not a simplification.

Legend for **Effect**: which way the assumption biases the headline result
(the anti-correlation between peak heat flux and bondline temperature).

---

## Atmosphere

| ID | Assumption | Justification | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-ATM-1 | US Standard Atmosphere 1976, 0–86 km, computed exactly from the standard's defined layer profile. | It is the reference standard for this class of study and is reproducible by anyone. | None on the mechanism. Real density varies by tens of percent with solar activity, season and latitude — quantified later under uncertainty propagation, not ignored. |
| A-ATM-2 | Above 86 km, values are **log-interpolated from a transcribed table** rather than computed. USSA-76 above 86 km requires a species-diffusion model with varying mean molar mass, which is out of scope. | Density above 86 km is ~10⁻⁶ kg/m³, so its contribution to **drag and to peak heat flux** is negligible. Its contribution to the **integrated** heat load is not — that was the original error in this row, corrected here. | **The "negligible" claim is only half true and the surviving half is now checked.** (i) Transcription: on 2026-09-20 the 90, 100, 110, 120 and 150 km rows were diffed against a **primary** copy of USSA-76 (NOAA-S/T 76-1562, Table I, pp. 68–69, read off 300 dpi renders) and agree **exactly to every printed digit**; nothing was changed. The 86, 95 and 130 km rows, the pressure column, and the log-interpolation *between* rows remain unchecked. Pinned by `tests/test_atmosphere.py::test_upper_table_against_primary_ussa76`. (ii) Magnitude: NR-14 shows 5–20% of a *feasible* design's integrated heat load — and hence its bondline temperature — accrues above 86 km, so "negligible" is false for the second objective. Still flagged `extrapolated=True` at runtime; gate G1A′ carries the split verdict. |
| A-ATM-3 | Model refuses to return values above 150 km. | Silent extrapolation is a worse failure than an exception. | None. |
| A-ATM-4 | Calorically perfect air (γ = 1.4) for speed of sound. | Valid for freestream in the homosphere. | Mach is used only as a bookkeeping quantity here. It is **not** valid behind the bow shock and must not be reused there. |
| A-ATM-5 | `ExponentialAtmosphere` (isothermal, ρ = ρ₀e^(−y/H)) exists in the package but is a **validation instrument, not a model of the air**. Added 2026-09-20 for gate G1B. | The Allen–Eggers closed form is derived for an exponential atmosphere and no other, so an integrator-vs-closed-form comparison is only a test of the integrator if both are handed the same exponential. | **None on any AETHER result.** No config selects it; `integrate_entry` defaults to USSA-76. `fit_exponential_to_us76` reports its own worst-case residual (>1.3× density ratio over 0–100 km) precisely so it is not mistaken for an improvement on USSA-76. |

## Trajectory

| ID | Assumption | Justification | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-TRAJ-1 | Point-mass 3-DOF, planar, **non-rotating** spherical Earth. | Standard for entry corridor and aerothermal sizing studies. | Earth rotation changes relative velocity by up to ~0.46 km/s at the equator, i.e. up to ~6% on V and therefore ~19% on q'' (V³). This shifts magnitudes; it does not reverse the ordering between a steep and a shallow entry. |
| A-TRAJ-2 | Constant C_D, no angle-of-attack dependence, no lift, no bank. | This is the Fidelity-0 baseline. Replacing it with a CFD-derived response surface is precisely what Fidelity 1 exists to do. | Real C_D falls through the transonic regime. The affected phase is below peak heating, so the peak-flux ordering is robust; the integrated load and hence the bondline result is more sensitive. |
| A-TRAJ-3 | Constant mass — no ablative mass loss. | Ablation chemistry is explicitly out of scope (spec §5). | An ablator carries energy away as mass loss, disproportionately in the long shallow case. This would **reduce** the size of the burn-vs-bake effect. It does not reverse it, because the mechanism is diffusive timescale, not magnitude. |
| A-TRAJ-4 | Integration stops at 20 km altitude. | Below this the aerothermal problem is over and descent/parachute phases (out of scope) take over. | The thermal solve continues past this point via the soak-out phase (A-TPS-5), so no bondline heating is lost. |
| A-TRAJ-5 | Gravity is **inverse-square**, g = μ/r², with the mean volumetric Earth radius 6371 km. `integrate_entry` gained optional `gravity_m_s2` and `planet_radius_m` overrides on 2026-09-20; both default to `None`, i.e. to the above. | A published reference case may have been computed with constant gravity (Putnam & Braun 2015 use g = 9.81 m/s², R = 6378 km). Reproducing it requires matching that choice rather than arguing about it. | **None on any AETHER result** — no config sets either override, and `test_production_default_is_unchanged_by_the_validation_hooks` asserts the default path is bit-identical. Matching the reference's gravity model moved the G1B agreement by up to 1.3 percentage points, which is the scale of the effect. |

## Aeroheating

| ID | Assumption | Justification | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-HEAT-1 | Sutton–Graves stagnation-point correlation, Earth-air constant k = 1.7415×10⁻⁴ SI in `q̇ = k√(ρ/R_n)V³`. | **NASA TR R-376 does not contain this equation or this constant.** It gives `q̇[MW/m²] = K√(p_s[atm]/R[m])·Δh[MJ/kg]` with K(air) = 0.1113 (Table II, p. 39, primary-verified). The V³ form is a later simplification. Carrying the algebra through in the report's own units gives **k = 1.74826×10⁻⁴, +0.39% from the value used** — full derivation in `docs/theory/sutton_graves_constant.md`. | **Constant deliberately unchanged** (NR-12): the substitutions needed to reach the V³ form are individually worse than 0.39% (Newtonian stagnation pressure −4.10%, cold wall −1.10%, dropped freestream enthalpy +0.81%), and the primary's own fit carries 3.3% average / 9.8% max error for air. **Treat this correlation as ±4% at best, and propagate that in M7 rather than assuming it away.** Model-form limits (catalycity, hot wall, radiation) are A-HEAT-2..4, not validation debt. |
| A-HEAT-2 | **Convective heating only.** Shock-layer radiative heating neglected. | Defensible for Earth entry at ~7.4 km/s with a metre-scale nose radius. | Would be indefensible at lunar-return speeds. Under-predicts total heating, more so for the steep/hot case — which makes the reported effect **conservative**. |
| A-HEAT-3 | **Cold-wall** correlation; no hot-wall or blowing correction. | Standard conservative practice. | Over-predicts flux into a hot surface. Conservative. |
| A-HEAT-4 | Stagnation point only; the whole TPS is analysed at the stagnation-point condition. | Worst-case point on the vehicle. | Real vehicles are frequently damaged at the shoulder or afterbody, which this model says nothing about. A genuine limitation, not a conservatism. |

## TPS

| ID | Assumption | Justification | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-TPS-1 | 1-D conduction normal to the surface. | TPS is thin relative to its lateral extent; lateral gradients are second-order. | Small. |
| A-TPS-2 | **Temperature-independent** k, ρ, c_p. | Fidelity-0 baseline; temperature-dependent properties are a documented later refinement. | Real insulator conductivity rises with temperature, which would **increase** bondline temperature in the long shallow case — i.e. strengthen the reported effect. |
| A-TPS-3 | **Adiabatic back face.** | Conservative: no heat is allowed to escape behind the structure. | Over-predicts bondline temperature for both cases. Does not change the ordering. |
| A-TPS-4 | Surface re-radiates to a 4 K sink with ε = 0.85, no radiation exchange between surfaces. | Near-vacuum entry environment. | Small. Radiation is the dominant surface cooling term and is modelled implicitly and nonlinearly, not linearised away. |
| A-TPS-5 | The conduction solve continues for **1200 s after the trajectory ends**, at zero incident flux. | The bondline peak *lags* the heat pulse. Truncating at the trajectory's end under-predicted the bondline peak by ~50 K in this study. | Essential. Without it the headline result is understated. Verified by `test_bondline_peak_lags_the_heat_pulse`. |
| A-TPS-6 | No contact resistance between layers; perfect thermal bonding. | Simplification. | Real contact resistance would lower bondline temperature. Affects both cases similarly. |
| A-TPS-7 | Material properties are **engineering placeholders** of the right order for a low-density insulator over an aluminium-like structure. They are not the properties of any specific qualified TPS material. | The result is about a *mechanism*, not about a product. | Absolute temperatures are indicative only. Any claim about a real material requires real property data. |
| A-TPS-8 | The conduction solver gained optional **bench boundary terms** on 2026-09-20 — front- and back-face convective loss, back-face re-radiation, and a series contact resistance at the flux-application plane — for the M8 coupon experiment. Every one of them **defaults to zero**, which reproduces A-TPS-3 and A-TPS-4 exactly. | A coupon in room air loses heat from both faces and is heated through a real interface. Calibrating against it without those terms would absorb the unmodelled losses into an apparent conductivity. | **None on any entry result.** The defaults are bit-identical to the previous behaviour, guarded by `test_defaults_reproduce_the_adiabatic_entry_model_exactly`. The terms are verified against closed-form cases in `tests/test_tps_boundaries.py` (gate G3b). |
| A-TPS-9 | With a non-zero contact resistance, the heater is modelled as a **thin film of negligible thermal mass**: surface losses are evaluated at the flux-application plane, which sits in front of the resistance. | Exact for a bonded polyimide film heater. | An approximation for a silicone pad and **wrong** for a metal spreader block, whose own heat capacity is not modelled. `experiments/thermal_coupon/README.md` §4 makes the heater choice an explicit student decision for this reason. |

## Geometry (Phase F)

| ID | Assumption | Justification | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-GEO-1 | The forebody is sphere-cone-torus-cone-flat-base. A pure spherical-segment forebody (Apollo-like) is represented as the LIMITING CASE where the straight fore-cone segment between the nose sphere and the shoulder torus has (numerically) zero length, not via a dedicated flag or shape variant. | One parametrisation covers both families (Apollo-like near-pure-sphere and Viking/Pathfinder-like sphere-cone) without a discrete mode switch that downstream code (CFD mesher, optimiser) would have to branch on. | None on validity. A design near the critical angle for its nose/diameter/shoulder combination is geometrically valid but numerically delicate (the fore-cone length is a small difference of two close quantities); `validate()`'s tolerance is scaled to the vehicle's own size for this reason. |
| A-GEO-2 | `cone_half_angle_deg` must be strictly in (0, 90) deg, enforced by `validate()` before any trigonometric derivation runs. Exactly 0 divides by zero in the fore-cone tangent-length formula. | The formula `(r2-r1)/tan(theta_c)` is undefined at theta_c=0; refusing the input is preferable to a silent inf/NaN propagating downstream. | None on the achievable design space - representing a pure spherical segment via A-GEO-1's limiting case does not require theta_c=0. |
| A-GEO-3 | `effective_nose_radius_m` (the quantity `heating.heat_flux_sutton_graves` consumes) is selected by `CapsuleGeometry.effective_nose_radius_model`. **`cap_radius`** is the original assumption, R_eff = R_n, and remains the dataclass DEFAULT so every pre-existing config and result is untouched. **`velocity_gradient`**, adopted 2026-09-20 and now the default in `configs/design_space.yaml`, takes R_eff from the measured stagnation-point velocity gradient (`src/aether/geometry/stagnation_gradient.py`). | `cap_radius` is exact only at the hemisphere. Sutton-Graves' 1/sqrt(R_n) is a statement about the stagnation VELOCITY GRADIENT, and only for a sphere is that gradient set by the nose radius; for a shallow cap it is governed by the body radius and the corner, so the benefit of flattening saturates. Keeping R_eff = R_n gave the optimiser an unbounded lever - flattening the nose to infinity removed 98.9% of the peak flux - which is the demonstrated O1 validation failure NR-15, and the only ground on which spec section 42 permits new physics. | **Direction: the old model UNDER-predicted heating on any shallow-cap design.** On the 64 M4 front designs R_eff is 0.685-0.693 of the cap radius, so peak flux rises 20.2-20.8% and peak bondline 6.7-9.1 K; all 64 stay feasible. Exact only at K = R_b/R_n = 1, where both models agree identically - which is why the baseline, M1 and M1b (a hemisphere-nosed body, R_n = D/2) are bit-for-bit unchanged. Carries the two primaries' own +-20% disagreement at K ~ 0.42 (about +-10% on flux), larger than A-HEAT-1's +-4%; propagate in M7. See A-GEO-3a and `docs/theory/effective_nose_radius.md`. |
| A-GEO-3a | The `velocity_gradient` model interpolates **NASA TN D-5121 (Ellison 1969) table I**, alpha = 0 rows, over K = R_b/R_n and R = R_c/R_b, with an exact hemisphere anchor at K = 1. Outside K <= 1 it falls back to R_eff = R_n and flags the result; R_c/R_b is clamped to the measured 0.4; a long straight fore-cone raises a note but changes no number. | Ellison is TABULATED (no figure read-off), is an experiment rather than a computation, and is the conservative of the two primaries. Neither source publishes a formula, so **the interpolation - shape-preserving cubic in K, linear in R - is this project's construction over their data points**, not a published fit. | Validity is alpha = 0, M >~ 3.5 (via Zoby & Sullivan's pressure-distribution invariance, which is what licenses using M = 8 tunnel data at 7.4 km/s), K in [0, 1], R_c/R_b <= 0.4. **Creates a new metric-gaming surface:** a rounder corner now raises stagnation heating, so an optimiser will want a sharp shoulder - which is exactly where real vehicles are damaged and where this model is silent (A-HEAT-4). It also makes `shoulder_ratio` a live variable (1.45% of peak flux across its box), so M4's screening decision to freeze it must be re-derived, not reused. |
| A-GEO-4 | The aft-cone/flat-base join (the base rim) is deliberately NOT tangent-continuous - a genuine geometric corner. | Real capsule aft-body rims (Apollo's aft heat shield edge included) are corners, not smooth blends; smoothing it would be an unjustified addition. | None on the physics modelled so far (Fidelity 0 heating is stagnation-point only, A-HEAT-4); becomes relevant once/if afterbody or base heating is ever modelled. |
| A-GEO-5 | `wetted_forebody_area_m2` is defined from the nose tip to the station of MAXIMUM diameter (the top of the shoulder torus), not to the shoulder/aft-cone tangent point or the full vehicle. | This is the conventional forward-facing heat-shield wetted area - the surface actually exposed to the bulk of stagnation-region heating. | Excludes the aft half of the shoulder and the whole afterbody from this area; a future afterbody-heating study needs its own area definition, not this one reused. |
| A-GEO-6 | `enclosed_volume_m3` sums exact closed forms for the nose spherical cap and both conical frusta with an adaptively-quadratured (`scipy.integrate.quad`) contribution from the toroidal shoulder (its contribution to `pi * int r(x)^2 dx` needs a `cos^3(psi)` term with no short closed form). | Keeps the two pieces that DO have textbook closed forms exact and tested directly against known values (hemisphere, cone, cylinder limits - `tests/test_geometry.py`), rather than making the whole quantity purely numerical. | Numerical only in the torus term, converged to quadrature tolerance (~1e-10 relative); cross-checked against an independent full numerical washer integration of `.profile()` to <1e-4 relative in tests, and against the exported STL's mesh volume (divergence theorem) to <1% at n_circumferential=96. |
| A-GEO-7 | `configs/geometry_bounds.yaml`'s numeric ranges (nose radius, diameter, shoulder radius, both cone angles, length, fineness ratio) are ENGINEERING PLACEHOLDERS for the order-of-magnitude design space of a sub-scale-to-crewed blunt Earth-entry capsule, informed by the general shape of flown sphere-cone vehicles (Apollo/Orion through Viking/Pathfinder). **Not sourced from a design standard.** | Spec S9: physical/design limits live in config, never hard-coded; an unsourced bound must say so rather than imply authority it doesn't have. | Same character as A-LIM-1/A-LIM-3: sets where the *feasible design space* falls for DOE/optimisation, not where pure geometric validity falls (that is hard-coded and unaffected by this file - `bounds={}` bypasses it entirely). Open item: needs justification from packaging/launch constraints or a named reference vehicle class, same as A-LIM-3. |
| A-GEO-8 | STL export is binary (little-endian IEEE-754), single traversal order (nose pole fan, ring-by-ring quad strips, base pole fan), no timestamps or host-dependent formatting. | Byte-for-byte determinism (spec's reproducibility requirement) needs a fixed writer, not merely a fixed geometry. | None on the geometry; a change to `n_circumferential`/`n_profile_points` changes the file (expected - it is a different discretisation), a change to neither does not. |
| A-GEO-9 | Watertightness (every mesh edge shared by exactly two triangles) and outward-normal orientation (positive mesh volume via the divergence theorem) are both verified by READING THE WRITTEN FILE BACK and checking, not assumed from the construction being "obviously" a closed ring topology. | A construction bug (off-by-one in ring indexing, wrong winding) would otherwise ship silently into whatever CFD mesher or slicer consumes the STL. | None - this is a verification discipline, not a modelling choice. Both checks are automated: `tests/test_geometry.py::test_stl_is_watertight`, `::test_stl_mesh_volume_matches_enclosed_volume`. |

## CFD (Fidelity 1) — added at M2

Full statement of permitted and forbidden uses: `docs/validation/M2_model_form_limits.md`.

| ID | Assumption | Justification | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-CFD-1 | **Calorically perfect air**, γ = 1.4, single species, no chemistry, no vibrational excitation, no radiation. | Spec §16: start with defensible compressible aerodynamics, not nonequilibrium chemistry. Validatable against exact relations and perfect-gas references. | Hypersonic *pressure* coefficients are insensitive to the gas model at the level of several percent (stagnation Cp ≈ 1.84 at γ = 1.4 against the Newtonian limit 2.0) — an argument from theory, **not validated here**, carried as an uncertainty. Shock stand-off and every shock-layer thermodynamic quantity are **wrong for flight** and must not be used as flight values. |
| A-CFD-2 | **Inviscid (Euler)**, slip walls, `rhoCentralFoam` with μ = 0. Chosen over laminar Navier–Stokes. | Blunt-forebody drag is pressure drag; a laminar boundary layer at flight Reynolds number would need wall-normal resolution the design-point budget cannot afford, would still not be turbulent, and would tempt someone to read a heat flux off it. Euler has exact and like-for-like references (Rayleigh pitot; Van Dyke & Gordon). | Omits skin friction (small for a blunt forebody; not quantified here). **No heat flux exists in this model** — heating stays with Sutton–Graves. |
| A-CFD-3 | **Axisymmetric, zero angle of attack, steady.** 5° wedge. | The Fidelity-0 trajectory is ballistic, C_L = 0. | No lift, moments or stability. Any α ≠ 0 question needs a 3-D case and its own validation. |
| A-CFD-4 | **Forebody-only domain**, nose to the maximum-radius station, zero-gradient outflow there. | An inviscid wake did not reach a steady state (NR-06) and has no physical base pressure. The outflow is supersonic, so the forebody solution cannot depend on what is downstream; the minimum outflow Mach number is measured on every case. | If a future geometry has a **subsonic** patch on that plane the boundary condition is ill-posed; the pipeline reports the number, and a value ≤ 1 must be treated as a failed case. |
| A-CFD-5 | **Base (afterbody) drag is NOT computed.** Fidelity 1 supplies C_D,fore (pressure, relative to p∞ on the base). | See A-CFD-4. | The trajectory needs a total C_D, so M3 must add a separately stated base-pressure assumption with its own uncertainty. For a sphere the verified numbers bound it: measured total C_D 0.940–0.944 at Mach 3.2–5.9 (Bailey & Hiatt, Re ≈ 10⁴) against Clark's forebody pressure drag evaluated at the same Mach numbers, 0.855–0.888, i.e. base + friction ≈ 5.5–9.4% of the total, shrinking with Mach number. A capsule's afterbody is not a sphere's; that range is an order of magnitude, not a model. **Open item for M3.** |
| A-CFD-6 | **OpenFOAM v2512**, not the v2606 the spec standardises on. | v2512 is what is installed (native macOS app build); nothing was installed or upgraded for M2. The exact version is recorded at run time in every report. | `rhoCentralFoam`, `blockMesh`, `extrudeMesh` and the function objects used are long-standing; no version-specific feature is relied on. Not verified against v2606 — re-run `make cfd-validate` if the install changes. |
| A-CFD-7 | Freestream static state p∞ = 1000 Pa, T∞ = 220 K for every case. | For the perfect-gas Euler equations C_D, p0/p∞ and Δ/R depend on Mach number and γ only. | None on any reported nondimensional number. They are **not** a flight condition. |
| A-CFD-8 | The computational domain's inflow boundary is placed using Billig's shock-shape correlation with a 2× stand-off margin and a 6° wider asymptote. | Keeps the bow shock inside the inflow boundary without meshing undisturbed freestream. | The correlation places the boundary only; it is never used to adjust a result. If a shock reached the boundary the solution would show it as a pressure rise on a fixed-value patch — check the flow-field figure for any new geometry class. |
| A-CFD-15 | **The stable Courant number depends on the mesh level.** Max Co 0.2 (declared after NR-07 on the coarse prototype) gives a force-converged solution on the coarse and medium meshes and a bounded limit cycle on the fine mesh; the fine-level solution of record is a restart of the fine case at max Co 0.1 (`courant_restarts` in `configs/cfd_validation.yaml`). The three levels of the mesh study therefore do NOT share one Courant number. | Under local time stepping the converged steady residual does not contain the time step, so a CONVERGED solution should not depend on it; measured: the Co 0.1 and Co 0.05 restarts agree to about 0.001% in C_D. | The window-mean of the Co 0.2 limit cycle differed from the converged value by about 0.03% - a third of the fine–medium difference - so a limit-cycling case's mean must not be used as if converged, which is what the unchanged criterion enforces. Any future mesh level or geometry class may need its own Courant limit; M3's six rejected capsule cases (NR-21) have not been retried this way. The rule was written after the original fine results were seen (NR-25). **Update 2026-09-21:** the six NR-21 cases HAVE now been retried this way (NR-27): four recovered, two did not. |
| A-CFD-16 | **Mach extension and per-shape top Mach (`cfd_surface_v2`).** Every anchor shape of the design is run once more at Mach 27 with the unchanged pipeline; the points are appended, so no v1 point id, fill point or held-out point changes. 9 of 14 are usable (NR-28). In the core range (Mach ≤ 20) a shape outside the hull is refused exactly as in v1 (A-AERO-1). Above it, a shape's C_D table stops at the last extension node still inside the hull and holds that value. | Rejecting a shape because a Mach-27 anchor near it failed would make v2 cover LESS of the design box than v1 for the sake of a node v1 did not have at all. Holding the top value is what v1 did at Mach 20, justified by Mach-number independence and measured at ≤ 0.74% between Mach 20 and 27. | The top Mach number, and so the heat-load share flown on a held value, differs from design to design (recorded per evaluation: `mach_range`, `mach_top_truncated_by_hull`, `aero_mach_top_of_table`, `aero_heat_fraction_above_cfd_mach`). Two neighbouring shapes can have tables ending at different Mach numbers - a discontinuity of at most the Mach-20-to-27 change in C_D (< 1%). Slender shapes (R_n/D ≈ 0.25–0.3, ~20°) stay at Mach ≈ 20. |
| A-CFD-17 | **Courant pass for capsule design points.** A case rejected ONLY for the force criterion is continued from its final solution as a new case at max Co 0.1, then 0.05: blocks of 5000 iterations, 2–8 blocks (M2's numbers), and it must meet the UNCHANGED criterion at the end of **two blocks in a row**; otherwise it stays REJECTED. | NR-25 showed the Courant number, not more waiting, removes a bounded C_D limit cycle. The two-in-a-row reading was made explicit in code after two slowly drifting cases met the criterion in one isolated window (NR-27). | Written after the v1 rejections and NR-25 had been seen, and tightened during the run - disclosed, and stricter in every respect. The criterion's 2000-iteration window cannot see slower oscillations: window-to-window mean agreement over the usable cases is 0.011% median, 0.22% worst (`diagnostic_window_mean_agreement_coarse.csv`). Usable cases now run at three different Courant numbers (0.2, 0.1, 0.05); for a converged local-time-stepping solution that should not matter (A-CFD-15). |

### OPEN STUDENT DECISION — the deceleration limit (raised 2026-09-20, not resolved)

The sourcing pass established that the 12 g in `configs/baseline.yaml` **cannot be
attributed to any document as a flat number** (A-LIM-1b). The value has deliberately
**not been changed**: which of the two readings below the project adopts is an engineering
choice about what vehicle and what mission this is, and that is the student's to make, not
an audit's. Both options are written out so the decision is made with its consequences
visible.

**Option A — keep 12 g, and relabel it.** Declare it an *emergency/off-nominal envelope*
value rather than a nominal design limit. It is defensible in that role: it sits inside
NASA-STD-3001's emergency-conditions envelope for pulses under ~2 minutes, and it matches
TM-104753's deconditioned 5-second plateau (12 G / 5 s) almost exactly. Costs nothing, and
every stored result stays valid. The honesty cost is that "feasible" then means *survivable
in a contingency*, not *acceptable for a nominal crewed entry*, and §39's final comparison
table must say so.

**Option B — adopt a duration-dependent check.** Replace the scalar with the
NASA-STD-3001 Table 6.5-1 deconditioned curve, evaluated against the design's own
acceleration pulse rather than its instantaneous peak (the standard's axis is *duration of
sustained acceleration*, which the evaluator does not currently compute — it stores
`max_g` only, so this needs a new metric before it can be applied).

**What it would do to the feasible region, measured — not estimated.** From the stored
M1b joint grid (`results/M1/M1-20260902T130547Z/joint_grid.csv`, 140 designs, max_g
spanning 8.93–22.60 g), counting designs that satisfy the g limit alone and both
constraints together:

| g limit | corresponding NASA-STD-3001 deconditioned duration | passes g limit | passes g **and** bondline ≤ 450 K |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12.0 (current) | — (not on the curve) | 48 / 140 (34.3%) | 21 / 140 (15.0%) |
| 10.0 | 10 s | 18 / 140 (12.9%) | 4 / 140 (2.9%) |
| 8.0 | 30 s | **0 / 140** | **0 / 140** |
| 6.3 | 50 s | 0 / 140 | 0 / 140 |
| 5.0 | 90 s | 0 / 140 | 0 / 140 |
| 4.0 | ≥150 s | 0 / 140 | 0 / 140 |

And the pulse this would be keyed against, from the baseline trajectory: peak 11.89 g,
with **25.5 s above 10 g, 40.0 s above 8 g, 52.6 s above 6.3 g and 64.1 s above 5 g**.
Reading those durations onto the deconditioned curve, the baseline exceeds it at every
level, not marginally.

**So the two options are not a tuning choice.** Option A keeps a feasible region of 15% of
the M1b grid. Option B, applied honestly to a deconditioned crew, **empties it** — the
entire M1b design space is infeasible for a nominal crewed entry, and the project's
burn-vs-bake result would have to be restated as being about an uncrewed or
contingency-entry vehicle, or the design space reopened (lower ballistic coefficient,
shallower entry, larger diameter than A-LIM-3/A-OPT-6 currently permit). That is a real
result and it should be decided deliberately rather than inherited from a placeholder.

Until it is decided, 12 g stands and A-LIM-1b says what it is and is not.

| A-AF-1 | **What the two fidelities are.** F0 = `evaluate_design` with the arm's current GP drag surface. F1 = one OpenFOAM case for the candidate's forebody shape through M3's design-point runner **unchanged** (shock-clearance checks, first-order start, M2's force-convergence criterion, domain retry, patience pass), on the **same mesh level as the surface**; a usable case joins the arm's training set, the surface is refitted (kernel re-optimised) and the candidate is re-evaluated. A case that fails the criterion is kept, logged, **costs a call** and yields no training point. | Spec §26, and the coordinator's definition. Promotion buys a locally better drag model whose benefit persists for later candidates — which is what CFD actually buys this project. | The evaluator's own `fidelity` column labels the aero model and is 1 on every M6 row; promotion is recorded in `eval_kind` and `promotion_log.json`. Mixing mesh levels in one GP is refused. |
| A-AF-2 | **Gate.** The F1 path refuses to run unless the starting surface was built under G4 = PASS **and** G4 reads PASS at launch. `allow_provisional: true` exists for smoke tests, is rejected in `mode: study`, and labels every artefact. Only `mode: study` with the real backend and a non-provisional gate publishes to `reports/`. | Spec §17. | No M6 number exists until G4 passes. |
| A-AF-3 | **Arms differ only in WHO is promoted.** Arms i–v share the search (M4's NSGA-II, population 20), the starting surface (rebuilt bit-for-bit), the seeded initial population, both budgets, and ONE promotion mechanism (Mach rule, charging, refit, charged re-evaluation). Comparators: random promotion and greedy promote-the-best on an even spending schedule; the whole CFD budget up front on a seeded Sobol design (what M3 did); no new CFD. | §26 "evaluate whether adaptive fidelity saves CFD calls" needs the saving to be attributable to the choice of candidate, not to a different Mach rule or a different search. | Seed-wise comparisons are paired. `ai_adaptive` (LLM search + the same policy, the agent's `requested_fidelity` as one input, same CFD cap) uses a different search and 2 seeds: exploratory, outside the criterion. |
| A-AF-4 | **The Mach rule.** Inside the hull a promotion is ONE case at the declared node (Mach 20/12/6/3) where heat-load weight × surface sigma is largest; weights are the baseline capsule's heat-load share by Mach band (M3 report §7). Outside the hull it is TWO cases, Mach max and Mach min — the only way a new shape enters the 4-D hull `evaluate_design` checks. A case within 0.02 (unit 4-D) of a training point is refused. | The hull is over (log Mach, shape) jointly and the evaluator needs the whole Mach range; near-duplicate CFD points made the GP overconfident (NR-22). | A hull extension costs a quarter of an 8-call budget. The weights are one capsule's, a declared heuristic. |
| A-AF-5 | **Probes are counted, capped, and not charged.** The policy pushes the surface's error bar through the canonical evaluator (`z_gp = −1, +1`, plus 0 under `on_extrapolation: flag` for an out-of-hull design): at most 4 candidates per batch. | A probe returns no new design — only the sensitivity of an already-submitted one to the surface's own error bar — and never enters a candidate log or a hypervolume. Charging it would make the policy pay search budget for looking at its own uncertainty, which the comparators never need to do. | The adaptive arms use more `evaluate_design` CPU than the others (reported per arm). If that is judged unfair the probes-per-arm column says by how much. |
| A-AF-6 | **Re-evaluation after a refit is charged**: the promoted design plus up to 8 believed-front members, 1 evaluation each. The sigma inflation factor is M3's k-fold value, inherited and not re-measured after a refit. | A re-evaluation is new information. Re-running a 5-fold CV inside every refit was judged not worth the complexity. | Promoting arms have fewer search evaluations. Belief about designs that were never refreshed is stale; this penalises promoting arms (the subject included). |
| A-AF-7 | **Scoring is on POOLED TRUTH**: one surface through the starting points plus every usable CFD point bought by any arm on any seed; hold-out cases excluded. An arm's score is the truth hypervolume of its **recommended set** (what its own final belief calls feasible and non-dominated); recommended designs that truth calls infeasible are dropped and counted. | An arm's own hypervolume is measured with its own, possibly flattering, surface. Scoring every design an arm evaluated would pay it for luck and hide the cost of a wrong surface, which is a wrong choice. | "Truth" is the best-known drag model, not CFD and not flight. Its error where the arms make their claims is measured by hold-out CFD cases fitted by no surface (1 per arm). Reported beside every score: belief HV, optimism gap, oracle HV, selection regret, false claims. |
| A-AF-8 | **Reference hypervolume** = pooled-truth HV of every truly feasible design of every arm and seed, plus a dedicated 2 × 1000-evaluation NSGA-II on the truth surface. Reference and ideal points are M4's (A-OPT-4). | The reference must not depend on how well the arms happened to cover the front, and pooled truth's hull contains every arm's hull, so designs only reachable by a hull extension are in it. | An arm that never promotes cannot reach a target that lies outside its hull, however converged it looks. Hull-rejected designs are re-scored and reported per arm. |
| A-AF-9 | **H2 criterion, pre-declared.** Cost of an arm-seed = CFD calls charged when its truth HV first reached 95 % of the reference (censored at budget + 1 if never). SUPPORTED iff the no-CFD arm does not reach the target in ≥ 3 of 5 seeds, `adaptive` does, its median cost is below the best non-adaptive CFD arm's and the exact one-sided permutation rank test gives p < 0.05. CONTRADICTED is the mirror image. If the no-CFD arm reaches the target the verdict is NOT_TESTABLE. 90 % and 98 % are reported as sensitivity, not as the criterion. | Spec §4 H2 / §26. "Fewer calls" is meaningless if no calls were needed. | 5 seeds, small-integer costs with ties: the test rejects only when samples barely overlap; a null is weak evidence of equivalence. Calls are spent while the search runs, so cost conflates CFD with search progress — the report shows both axes. |
| A-AF-10 | **Study size.** 5 seeds × 8 CFD calls × 4 CFD arms = 160 calls (+16 for `ai_adaptive`, +6 hold-out), 200 F0 evaluations per arm-seed, at most 3 serial solvers side by side. Estimated 3.3–5.1 h from M3's measured coarse-mesh case times. | Machine time was capped at 4–5 h; seeds were kept at 5 (with 3 seeds the exact test cannot reach p < 0.05) and the per-arm CFD budget was cut instead. | 8 calls is coarse. On a medium-mesh surface the same study is ~8× longer and must be cut. |


## Uncertainty propagation and robust design (M7) — added 2026-09-20

Everything here is declared in `configs/uncertainty.yaml`, with each input's source line and
tier beside it, before any study is run. **The study has not been run**; these are the rules
it will be run and scored by. The full inventory is not repeated here — the config is the
inventory and it is snapshotted with every run. Theory, written to be defended:
`docs/theory/uncertainty.md`.

| ID | Assumption | Justification | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-UQ-1 | **Every uncertain input is labelled ALEATORY or EPISTEMIC and the two are carried separately to the outputs.** Aleatory = mission-to-mission variability (the day's atmosphere, as-built mass, delivered flight-path angle). Epistemic = ignorance about something that does not vary at all (which of two NASA reports is right; what the placeholder TPS material actually is). The default sampling mode is NESTED — E epistemic branches × A aleatory draws sharing common random numbers — so each branch yields a complete output distribution and the report shows a p-box rather than one curve. | They demand different responses. Variability is absorbed by margin and no study removes it; ignorance is removed by evidence and margin is the expensive way to handle it. Pooling them destroys exactly the information that says which to buy. | The pooled mean and s.d. ARE also reported, combined by the law of total variance — i.e. **in quadrature**, which the code says in those words. That identity is exact for the sampled mixture but is only meaningful if an epistemic band is read as a probability distribution, which it is not. The `band` (p-box) view is the primary result and does not require that reading. |
| A-UQ-2 | **Source tiering is enforced by the loader, not by convention.** Every input carries a `source` line and a tier (`T1` primary opened and read / `T2` reputable secondary / `T3` engineering judgment), on the same scale as `docs/validation/sourcing_report.md`. An input tiered `T3` is REFUSED unless its own source line contains the word "judgment". | Spec §9 and A-LIM-2: an unsourced value is an open question, not a pass. An unsourced spread is allowed here; what is not allowed is an unsourced spread that reads as though it were sourced. | At Fidelity 0, **7 of 9 active inputs are T3**. The two T1 inputs — the effective-nose-radius model form and the Sutton–Graves constant — are the ones this project measured about itself, and they are not the small ones. |
| A-UQ-3 | **An input whose evidence does not exist REFUSES; it does not silently vanish.** Each requirement carries its own policy. `cd_discretisation` requires M2's GCI (`results/M2/<run>/gci.csv`) and declares `refuse`, so selecting the CFD drag surface stops the run rather than propagating one fewer C_D term. The same input's `aero_model` requirement declares `skip`, because at Fidelity 0 there is no surface for the term to be about. | A-CFD-9, verbatim: "Until M2 has a three-level GCI the band is unavailable, and sampling it raises rather than returning zero." Dropping a term understates the uncertainty and nothing in the output would show it. | Verified today: with `vehicle.aero.model: cfd_surface_v1` the loader raises `InputUnavailable` naming the input, the requirement and `gci.csv`. Pinned by `tests/test_uncertainty.py::test_the_run_refuses_when_the_gci_term_is_unavailable`. **Update 2026-09-21:** with `cfd_surface_v2` selected and M2's GCI present, the loader now builds all four C_D terms and skips only `cd_fidelity0_judgment` (`scripts/check_study_preconditions.py`); the refusal path is pinned hermetically by the same test. |
| A-UQ-ATM-1 | **The density dispersion is applied as an ISOTHERMAL perturbation**: density and pressure are multiplied by the same altitude-dependent factor and temperature is left alone, so `p = ρRT` holds exactly and the speed of sound — hence the Mach number at a given altitude and velocity — is unchanged. | Every quantity this project reports (drag, dynamic pressure, deceleration, Sutton–Graves flux) depends on density and velocity, not on the speed of sound. | Real upper-atmosphere variability is substantially THERMAL, and a thermal anomaly would also move the speed of sound by roughly √(δT/T). Second-order here; it would **not** be at Fidelity 1, where C_D is a function of Mach. Stated, not modelled. |
| A-UQ-ATM-2 | **One density z-score is drawn per trajectory**, not per integration step, so the anomaly is perfectly correlated in altitude. | A density anomaly is a weather-like field correlated over hundreds of kilometres; an entry lasts minutes and crosses one such field. Sampling independently at each step would model it as white noise, which averages out and UNDER-states its effect on integrated quantities. Same argument as A-AERO-2 for the GP draw. | Perfect vertical correlation is the CONSERVATIVE choice for trajectory-integrated quantities, which is what the bondline responds to. Real dispersion has a finite vertical correlation length; that structure is not modelled. |
| A-UQ-ATM-3 | **The 1-σ density profile is mixed-tier and its weakest rows govern it.** 0–85 km: ✅ T1 standard deviations from NASA's own GRAM validation against Shuttle re-entry data (NASA-TM-4715 Tables 3.1 p. 19 and 3.3 p. 21, observed; NASA/TM-20210022157 pp. 63–65). ≥ 90 km: ❌ T3 — **no document opened in the sourcing pass states a 1-σ density dispersion above 86 km**, so those rows convert the primary OBSERVED RANGES of the U.S. Standard Atmosphere Supplements 1966 (NTRS 19670028571, p. 28 falling-sphere data; p. 5) into a 1-σ proxy. That conversion is this project's step. | An entry study needs a dispersion above 86 km whether or not one has been published as a σ; the alternative is to set it to zero, which is a stronger and worse claim. | **Material, not cosmetic.** NR-14 measured that 5–20% of a feasible design's integrated heat load accrues above 86 km. Cross-check, stated rather than hidden: Stardust's operations analysis (Desai et al., AIAA 2006-6410 p. 5 / fig. 5) quotes a GRAM-95 3-σ envelope of ±5% to ±25% across its whole entry profile, i.e. 1.7–8.3% at 1-σ — consistent with the rows below 86 km and much smaller than those above. NRLMSISE-00's published model-vs-data s.d. (Picone et al., JGR 107(A12) 2002, archive tables) independently indicate ~17–36% over 80–120 km, corroborating the size; those digits came off an OCR'd table and the conversion from log-density units was the reader's, so it is 🟡 T2 corroboration and not the source. GRAM-99's 3.0–8.0% is stated for **≥ 200 km** and is deliberately NOT extrapolated downwards. |
| A-UQ-NOSE-1 | **The effective-nose-radius uncertainty is a DISCRETE model-form switch between the two primaries**, not Gaussian noise: `velocity_gradient` (Ellison, NASA TN D-5121, adopted) vs `velocity_gradient_zoby` (the same, displaced by Ellison's own published disagreement with Zoby & Sullivan, NASA TM X-1067), 50/50. The displacement is K-dependent: 10% at K = 0, 20% at K = 0.417, 10% at K = 0.707, 0 at K = 1 — Ellison p. 5 verbatim, plus an exact anchor at the hemisphere. Its SIGN is evidenced: at the flat face Ellison tabulates R_eff = 3.155 R_b against Zoby & Sullivan's digitised 3.40–3.50 R_b, so the other primary's R_eff is larger and its heating lower, which is why Ellison is the conservative source. | Two reports disagree; one is closer to right; there is no continuum between them and no frequency to be normal about. A discrete switch is what the evidence IS. R_eff enters as 1/√R_eff, so the ~20% disagreement at K ≈ 0.42 — exactly where the M4 front sits — is about ±10% on heat flux, against the Sutton–Graves constant's own ±4%. | **This is the dominant heating uncertainty and the reason M7 has the shape it does.** Three ways the alternative state is WIDER than the source strictly licenses, all deliberate: (i) "within 10 percent" is an upper BOUND in Ellison and is used here as the value; (ii) the 20% is stated only at (K = 0.417, R = 0) and is applied here at every corner ratio; (iii) the shape between the three stated K values is this project's PCHIP interpolation. At K = 1 the two agree identically by Zoby & Sullivan's eq. (5), so a zero sensitivity index on the reference capsule is geometry, not a broken input — the report computes K and says so. The 50/50 split is declared indifference between two primaries, not a measured frequency. |
| A-UQ-HI86-1 | **The >86 km heating model-form term multiplies the HEAT FLUX above 86 km, not the density**, over a declared ±50% band (❌ T3 judgment). | It is a statement about how wrong a continuum stagnation-point correlation is where the flow is transitional to rarefied and the atmosphere table is interpolated (G1A′ `LIMITED`), which is a different question from how the air varies — `atmospheric_density` is that question. | A-UQ-CD-1 | **At Fidelity 0 the C_D uncertainty is one unsourced ±10% band on a constant**, replacing the four sourced Fidelity-1 terms (GP predictive σ with its stored 1.65 inflation; the M2 discretisation band; the base-drag band; the declared perfect-gas half-band), which do not exist when the aerodynamics are `constant`. | A-UQ-ROB-1 | **The robust problem is declared in config before any run**: minimise the 95th percentile of each O1 objective (empirical order-statistic estimator), subject to P(violation) ≤ 0.05 per constraint. A draw that produced no physics counts as a violation of EVERY chance constraint and is reported separately. | Spec §28. A design is judged on a bad day, not an average one; a vehicle that does not complete an entry has satisfied nothing. Declaring the definition beforehand is what stops a percentile being chosen after seeing a front. | With an inner sample of S draws the estimated probability can only take values in multiples of 1/S, so α = 0.05 at S = 32 means **"at most 1 of 32 draws may violate"**, and an α below 1/S would silently mean ZERO violations. `RobustSettings.effective_chance_rule` computes and prints that translation. |
| A-UQ-ROB-2 | **The shortcut: a modest inner sample (S = 32) under COMMON RANDOM NUMBERS** — the same draws for every candidate in every generation — instead of full nested Monte Carlo, which would be ~10 h per seed on this machine. Without it the search would chase sampling noise. | A 95th percentile from 32 draws is noisy and biased inward, and CRN buys precision on the comparison at the price of correlated error on the level. **Neither is assumed:** `verify_shortcut` re-scores a subset of the finished front under full independent Monte Carlo (1000 draws, different seed, no CRN) and reports the measured bias, the rank correlation and the violation-probability error against tolerances declared before the run.
