## 0. What this run is, and is not

This is an uncertainty study of a **model**, not of a vehicle. It propagates the spread that `configs/uncertainty.yaml` declares, through the assumptions `ASSUMPTIONS.md` declares. It does not and cannot cover a model-form error that is not on that list — most importantly gate **G2′** (Sutton–Graves' catalycity, hot-wall and radiation assumptions, where catalycity alone is worth about a factor of two) is `LIMITED` and untouched here. An output distribution from this study is therefore a **lower bound on the real uncertainty**, and the §7 limitations section lists what is missing rather than leaving it implied.

**The pre-declared check is the ROW bootstrap**, as `configs/uncertainty.yaml` declared it before the run, and its verdict is the only verdict here: all reported statistics are within the declared tolerance on it.

**That check understates the sampling error, and the second pair of columns is why (NR-34 item 2).** The draw set is nested: 24 epistemic branches × 125 shared aleatory draws, so the 3000 rows are not independent - every row of a branch shares one value of every epistemic input, and 97.2%–98.7% of this output's variance is between branches (§3.1 decomposition). A bootstrap must resample the unit that was independently drawn. Resampling whole branches (24 clusters, 4000 resamples, seed 20260927) gives half-widths 9–18 times the row bootstrap's. This second bootstrap was added after the run; it is reported beside the pre-declared check, never instead of it, and it changes no verdict.

Applying the declared tolerance to the branch bootstrap - the stricter reading, not the declared one - the `baseline` mean misses it (1.06% = ±5.3 K against 1.00%); the other 7 of 8 statistics meet it. So "converged to a few hundredths of a percent" is not a fair summary of this table: absolute means and percentiles here are known to roughly the branch-bootstrap half-width. More epistemic branches, not more draws, is what would tighten them.

## 7. Limitations

**7 of 12 active inputs are engineering judgment** (`atmospheric_density`, `vehicle_mass`, `entry_flight_path_angle`, `high_altitude_heating_model`, `tps_conductivity`, `tps_specific_heat`, `cd_perfect_gas_model_form`). Their spreads are declared, not sourced, and the width of every output distribution here is conditional on them. 4 inputs are tiered ✅ T1 primary (`effective_nose_radius_model`, `sutton_graves_coefficient`, `cd_surrogate_gp`, `cd_discretisation`). The largest total-order index any of them reaches in §4 is 0.706 (`effective_nose_radius_model`, design `joint_knee`, `peak_heat_flux_w_m2`).

**Drag uncertainty.** This run is Fidelity 1 (`cfd_surface_v2`), and 4 C_D terms were propagated: `cd_surrogate_gp` (✅ T1 primary), `cd_discretisation` (✅ T1 primary), `cd_perfect_gas_model_form` (❌ T3 engineering judgment), `cd_base_drag` (🟡 T2 secondary). Declared but not active: `cd_fidelity0_judgment` - needs vehicle.aero.model == 'constant', but the base config uses 'cfd_surface_v2'.

**Not covered at all.** Gate G2′ (aeroheating model form: catalycity, hot wall, radiation) is `LIMITED` and is not in the inventory; catalycity alone is reported at roughly a factor of two. A-TPS-2's temperature-independent conductivity, A-TPS-3's adiabatic back face, A-HEAT-4's stagnation-point-only treatment and A-TRAJ-1's non-rotating Earth are structural model-form choices with no distribution attached. The deceleration limit itself is an **open student decision** (A-LIM-1b): 12 g corresponds to no documented sustained-g curve, and adopting the NASA-STD-3001 deconditioned curve would empty the feasible region. Every chance constraint on `max_g` below is therefore a statement about a placeholder limit.

**The atmosphere above 86 km.** Gate G1A′ is `PASS` for five transcribed rows and `LIMITED` for everything else, the interpolation between rows is not the species-diffusion model USSA-76 actually defines there, and NR-14 measured that 5–20% of a feasible design's heat load comes from that band. Two inputs here — the >86 km density dispersion rows and the high-altitude heating multiplier — are engineering judgment about exactly that region, so the study quantifies the consequence of a known weakness without removing it.
