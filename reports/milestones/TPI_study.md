# Thermal Penetration Index — does it earn its place?
> Run `TPI-20260920T124732Z` · git `046e0a1` · **working tree dirty** · config hash `bbdd1b294619` · generated by `scripts/run_tpi_study.py`
>
> Regenerate with `make tpi`. Raw data: `results/TPI/TPI-20260920T124732Z/tpi_grid.csv`, `results/TPI/TPI-20260920T124732Z/summary.json`. Every number below is read from those files.

## Question
Spec §44 proposes a Thermal Penetration Index: a weighted double integral, over TPS depth and over time, of temperature exceedance above a reference temperature.

```
TPI = ∫₀^t_end ∫₀^L  w(x) · max( T(x,t) − T_ref , 0 )  dx dt       [K·m·s]
```

The project already reports peak bondline temperature and integrated external heat load. A third number is only worth carrying if it would make a designer choose differently. The test is therefore not *is TPI physically meaningful* — it plainly is — but **does its ordering of designs contain anything the existing metrics do not**.

## Method
The index was evaluated over the M1b design grid: 14 entry flight-path angles × 10 capsule diameters = **140 coupled evaluations**, all through the canonical `evaluate_design` path, of which 21 satisfy every hard constraint.

Reference configuration: `uniform · Tref=400K · x<=15mm`. Depth integration is restricted to the insulator (15 mm); the aluminium structure behind the bondline is not TPS and its thermal mass would otherwise dominate the index.

**Redundancy criterion, declared in `configs/tpi_study.yaml` before the run:** TPI is discarded if the rank-space R² of TPI regressed on (peak bondline temperature, integrated heat load) is ≥ **0.98**, or if |Spearman ρ| against peak bondline temperature alone is ≥ **0.99**.

## Guard against the known trap
M1 recorded a Spearman ρ of −1.000 between peak flux and bondline temperature and then said plainly that the number was close to content-free: over a **one-parameter** sweep, two strictly monotone functions of that parameter can only give ρ = ±1, whatever the physics. The same trap is live here.

Along each fixed-diameter line of this grid (14 points, flight-path angle varying alone), 10 of 10 lines give |ρ| ≥ 0.999 between TPI and peak bondline temperature; the smallest |ρ| on any line is 1.0000. **Those line correlations are uninformative and are reported here only to show why the 2-D number is the one that counts.** Every correlation quoted below is over the full 2-D grid, where the metrics are not forced into a common ordering.

## Result
TPI over the grid spans 587.1 – 3648 K·m·s.

### Where the index comes from — and a prediction that was half wrong
`docs/theory/tpi.md` §5 predicted, before the run, that the depth integral would be *dominated by the outer millimetre or two*. **The first half of that is right and the second half is not,** and the distinction turns out to be the real explanation for the verdict.

Right: the unweighted time-integrated exceedance does peak at the surface — at a depth between 0.062 and 0.062 mm, the shallowest cell in the mesh, for every one of the 140 designs.

Wrong: it does not *dominate*. The outermost 20% of the TPS depth carries only 27.6–40.6% of the integral, against the 20% a perfectly uniform profile would give. The profile declines smoothly with depth; it is not a surface spike.

The actual mechanism is the profile's **shape invariance**. Normalised to unit length, the exceedance profiles of the 140 designs that exceed T_ref anywhere have a minimum pairwise cosine similarity of **0.9366** (mean 0.9891). Every design produces very nearly the *same* depth profile scaled by a different factor. TPI is therefore that scalar — and so is any weighted integral of the same profile, which is why **no weighting family rescues it**: the sensitivity table below shows all 16 configurations failing the redundancy criterion together. A metric cannot separate designs along a dimension in which the designs do not differ.
See `reports/figures/TPI_weighting_and_profile.png` for the profiles themselves.

## Verdict: **DISCARD**

- TPI's ranking is 99.94% reconstructible from peak bondline temperature and integrated heat load (rank R^2 = 0.9994 >= 0.98), so it restates information the project already reports.

- TPI and peak bondline temperature are ordered almost identically over the 2-D grid (Spearman rho = +0.9992, |rho| >= 0.99).

- A TPI-minimising optimiser selects the same design as a bondline-minimising one, so adopting TPI would not have changed any design decision in this study.

### What was NOT claimed
No claim is made that TPI is a new aerospace standard, and none that it is original. `docs/theory/tpi.md` reviews the prior art for cumulative thermal-exposure metrics — integrated heat load in TPS sizing, CEM43 thermal dose in hyperthermia, Arrhenius damage integrals — and TPI sits inside that family rather than beside it.

## Limitations
- This verdict is about **this design space**: one vehicle family, two design variables, a constant-property two-layer stack, Fidelity 0 aerodynamics. A stack with a genuinely different in-depth response — a charring ablator, a temperature-dependent conductivity, a multi-layer insulator with an interior bondline — could decouple TPI from peak bondline temperature. That is a testable prediction, not a defence of the metric.
- The redundancy criterion is a threshold on a rank R². It was declared in advance, but it is still a threshold, and a metric just below it would not thereby be useful.
- T_ref and the weighting family were swept over declared ranges, not optimised. A configuration outside those ranges could behave differently; the ranges are in the config snapshot.
