## Question
Does minimising peak external heat flux also minimise the temperature reached at the TPS bondline?

## Method
Entry flight-path angle was swept over [-8.00, -1.50] degrees in 41 steps. Every other design variable was held fixed at the baseline configuration, so any difference between candidates is attributable to trajectory shape alone. Each candidate ran the full Fidelity-0 chain (USSA-76 atmosphere → 3-DOF trajectory → Sutton–Graves stagnation heating → 1-D transient multilayer conduction with a radiating surface and a post-entry soak-out). No candidate was discarded.

## Result
**H0 is supported in the tested domain.**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Candidates evaluated | 41 |
| Signature counterexample pairs | 818 |
| Entry angle minimising peak flux | -1.50° (142.38 W/cm²) |
| Entry angle minimising bondline temperature | -8.00° (423.7 K) |
| Peak-flux range across sweep | 142.38 – 230.64 W/cm² |
| Bondline-temperature range across sweep | 423.7 – 537.8 K |

### The two metrics are optimised by different trajectories
The entry angle that minimises peak heat flux (-1.50°) is **not** the one that minimises bondline temperature (-8.00°). Designing against peak flux alone therefore selects the wrong trajectory for the failure mode that actually matters.

### Strongest counterexample
| | A (higher peak flux) | B (lower peak flux) |
|---|---|---|
| Entry angle | -8.00° | -1.50° |
| Peak heat flux | 230.64 W/cm² | **142.38 W/cm²** |
| Integrated external load | 79.3 MJ/m² | 128.3 MJ/m² |
| Entry duration | 134.8 s | 324.1 s |
| **Peak bondline temperature** | 423.7 K | **537.8 K** |

Candidate B reduces peak heat flux by **38.3%** — a large win by the conventional metric — while making the bondline **114.1 K hotter**.

## Mechanism
The shallower entry decelerates in thinner air over a longer time. Peak flux falls (q'' ∝ √ρ·V³), but the pulse lengthens from 135 s to 324 s and the integrated external load rises from 79.3 to 128.3 MJ/m². The TPS is a diffusive low-pass filter: its characteristic time L²/α is long compared with a steep entry but *comparable* to a shallow one. A short intense pulse is absorbed near the surface and re-radiated away at T⁴ before it can diffuse inward; a long mild pulse has time to reach the bondline.

Peak flux is a **surface** quantity. Bondline temperature is an **integrated, delayed** quantity. Optimising the first does not constrain the second.

## The anti-correlation is global, not a corner case
Rank correlation between peak heat flux and peak bondline temperature across the sweep is **Spearman rho = -1.000**. A value of exactly -1 means the ordering is perfectly reversed: *every* candidate with a lower peak heat flux than another has a higher bondline temperature than it. The counterexample is not a pathological pair hiding in the domain - the entire domain is a counterexample.

## The feasibility squeeze
Of 41 candidates, **0 satisfy every hard constraint**. Steep entries violate the deceleration limit; shallow entries violate the bondline limit; nothing in between satisfies both. With the geometry frozen, trajectory shape alone cannot produce a valid design. This is not a modelling failure - it is the result that motivates O1, which optimises geometry and trajectory *jointly* rather than sequentially.

## M1b — opening the geometry axis (H1)
A full-factorial grid over entry angle and capsule diameter (140 coupled evaluations, mass held constant so diameter sets the ballistic coefficient, bluntness ratio held constant so the optimiser cannot cheat the heating correlation) recovers a feasible region: **21 of 140 designs satisfy every constraint**.

Two optimisers were then run over that feasible set. One minimises peak heat flux, as conventional practice does. One respects O1 and treats bondline temperature as an objective in its own right.

| | Baseline | Peak-flux-only optimum | Joint O1 optimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry angle γ₀ | -3.00° | -1.50° | -3.50° |
| Diameter | 1.20 m | 3.00 m | 3.00 m |
| Ballistic coefficient β | 257.9 | 41.3 | 41.3 kg m⁻² |
| Peak heat flux | 160.28 | **37.01** | 44.27 W cm⁻² |
| Integrated load | 112.5 | 32.1 | 26.5 MJ m⁻² |
| Peak surface temperature | 2381 | 1626 | 1699 K |
| **Peak bondline temperature** | 494.8 | 444.1 | **411.6 K** |
| Bondline margin to limit | -10.0% | +1.3% | +8.5% |
| Max deceleration | 11.89 | 9.13 | 11.69 g |
| Feasible | False | True | True |

The joint optimum runs its bondline **32.5 K cooler** than the design a peak-flux-only search selects, at a cost of +19.6% on peak heat flux. In margin terms the peak-flux-only design sits close enough to the bondline allowable that ordinary uncertainty in material conductivity or atmospheric density could push it over; the joint design does not. **H1 is supported.**

Note that both optima choose the largest available diameter, i.e. the lowest ballistic coefficient. That is the expected physics — a lower β decelerates higher in thinner air — and it means the diameter bound, not the physics, is active. A real study must either justify that bound from packaging/launch-vehicle constraints or widen it. Recorded as an open item in `PROJECT_STATUS.md`.

## Limitations of this result
This is a Fidelity-0 result and is only as good as its assumptions:

- Constant C_D. A real capsule's drag coefficient varies with Mach and angle of attack; that is what Fidelity 1 (OpenFOAM) exists to supply. The *direction* of the effect is robust to this, the magnitudes are not.
- Sutton–Graves is a cold-wall, convective-only, stagnation-point correlation. Radiative heating and hot-wall correction are absent.
- No ablation. A real ablator would blunt the shallow case by carrying energy away as mass loss, reducing but not reversing the effect.
- Adiabatic back face, constant material properties, no contact resistance.
- Single-parameter sweep. The counterexample is demonstrated over trajectory shape only; the geometry axis is not yet exercised.

The claim supported by this milestone is narrow and deliberately so: *within this model, peak heat flux and bondline temperature are optimised by different trajectories.* No claim is made about any flight vehicle.
